# Prohibited pattern catalogue

Apply every entry to original English prose. The examples illustrate prohibited uses. Do not imitate their disallowed wording. Each pattern defines an editorial constraint and supplies no proof of AI authorship. Read the conditions before flagging an isolated word. Protected quotations follow R15. Research support and policy choices appear in [evidence](evidence.md).

## Find a pattern

| Editing problem | Patterns |
| --- | --- |
| Framing that delays or replaces the point | P01–P06 |
| Sentence structures that obscure the relation | P07–P11 |
| Claims without the necessary support | P12–P15 |
| Narration and closure imposed on the material | P16–P20 |
| Repetition across sentences or paragraphs | P21–P22 |

Each worked example uses an authored fictional draft. The brief disallowed example isolates the trigger. A revised passage demonstrates a faithful repair; a response outside the deliverable identifies a consequential ambiguity and may ask a real clarification question. Before deleting any evaluation, apply R02–R03 to distinguish empty framing from the author's substantive judgment.

## P01 — Negative redefinition

**Trigger:** Reject a label and substitute another through contrast: “not X but Y,” “less X than Y,” “X? No, Y,” reversed clauses, or a denial followed by a corrective sentence.

**Disallowed example:** “The delay is not a failure. It is an opportunity.”

**Repair:** State the supported positive claim directly. Preserve a consequential factual negation in a separate explanation without a staged redefinition.

**Boundary:** A necessary factual denial without a corrective rhetorical frame remains valid; exact quotations remain protected.

**Worked draft:** The delay is not a failure; it is an opportunity. The team will use the extra day to check the calculations.

**Response outside the deliverable:** Does “failure” refer to a missed requirement, or express your assessment of the delay? The planned check supports a use for the extra day. I need the intended meaning of “failure” to recast the judgment faithfully.

**Preservation check:** The draft rejects a judgment of failure and evaluates the delay as an opportunity. Removing both judgments would change the writer's position. Clarification precedes a completed rewrite; the extra day and planned check remain required content.

## P02 — Negative additive upgrade

**Trigger:** Escalate a claim through “not only,” “not just,” “not merely,” “more than just,” or “does more than” followed by a second elevated characterization.

**Disallowed example:** “The archive does more than preserve records; it transforms memory.”

**Repair:** Recast the functions and judgments directly. Explain a mechanism only when the material supplies it. If an elevated characterization carries the author's position, preserve it or clarify its intended meaning and warrant before changing it.

**Boundary:** Ordinary quantitative comparisons remain valid.

**Worked draft:** The archive does more than preserve records; it transforms access. Readers can search its dated entries by name.

**Response outside the deliverable:** What change in access does “transforms access” describe, and what supports that assessment? The draft supplies preservation and name-based search, but leaves the comparison behind the transformation claim unspecified.

**Preservation check:** Keep the preservation function, dated entries and search field. The author's assessment of transformed access also counts as content. Its missing explanation requires clarification; deleting it would silently narrow the argument.

## P03 — Empty parallel framing and forced triads

**Trigger:** Use mirrored clauses, repeated grammatical openings, balanced slogans or a three-part sequence to simulate development while repeating a proposition or supplying unsupported significance. Check adjacent and distant sentences. Grammatical symmetry alone does not trigger this rule.

**Disallowed example:** “It shapes memory, it shapes identity, it shapes history.”

**Repair:** Remove repeated propositions or clarify the missing support. Preserve useful parallelism when distinct clauses express an actual comparison, condition or cumulative inference. Do not disguise a repeated claim by splitting its clauses or swapping synonyms.

**Boundary:** Necessary enumeration, balanced comparisons and the course's cumulative sentence exercise remain valid when each element contributes distinct meaning. Do not force asymmetry into clear reasoning.

**Worked draft:** The review took two days. It required two days of review. It occupied a two-day review period.

**Revised prose:** The review took two days.

**Preservation check:** All three sentences state the same duration. Deleting the duplicates loses no distinct claim. A sequence containing three different supplied actions would require preserving all three.

## P04 — Empty question-and-answer staging

**Trigger:** Add a generic question that announces importance, fabricates reader interest or merely repeats what the next sentence states. Repeated question-and-answer frames also trigger review when they replace analysis.

**Disallowed example:** “Why does this matter? The answer lies in memory.”

**Repair:** Remove the empty question and develop the specific issue. Retain a precise question when the passage investigates it through evidence, competing interpretations or a consequential uncertainty.

**Boundary:** The source draft recommends question-led sections and open-ended questions. Those devices remain valid when they organize genuine analysis. A question may receive an answer in the following prose; the presence of an answer alone does not make it formulaic. Clarification questions and exact quoted questions also remain valid.

**Worked draft:** Why does the deadline matter? The printer needs the approved file by Tuesday to prepare the copies for Friday.

**Revised prose:** The printer needs the approved file by Tuesday to prepare the copies for Friday.

**Preservation check:** The dependency replaces the rhetorical opener.

## P05 — Staged importance

**Trigger:** Announce significance or attention before naming a substantive claim.

**Disallowed example:** “It is important to note that this finding has profound implications.”

**Repair:** Give the finding and its specific supported consequence.

**Boundary:** An evidence-backed evaluation of significance may remain if it adds information.

**Worked draft:** It is important to note that the second batch arrived two days late.

**Revised prose:** The second batch arrived two days late.

**Preservation check:** The sentence states the consequential fact without announcing importance.

## P06 — Fabricated reader objection

**Trigger:** Invent a predictable skeptical reader to stage a rebuttal.

**Disallowed example:** “You might be wondering whether this really works.”

**Repair:** Address the evidenced limitation directly. Attribute actual objections when supplied.

**Boundary:** Analyze a supplied person's actual objection accurately.

**Worked draft:** You might be wondering whether the count includes duplicates. The count excludes duplicate entries.

**Revised prose:** The count excludes duplicate entries.

**Preservation check:** The revision invents no reader or objection.

## P07 — Evaluative participial rider

**Trigger:** Append an -ing clause that declares importance, complexity, or wider meaning without a supported inference.

**Disallowed example:** “The number rose, highlighting the profound complexity of the issue.”

**Repair:** Supply the warrant from the provided material. Remove empty evaluation only when the deletion preserves meaning under R03; flag missing support for a substantive judgment.

**Boundary:** A participial clause describing a supplied action or consequence may remain.

**Worked draft:** Enrollment rose from 20 to 24, highlighting a profound transformation in the program.

**Response outside the deliverable:** What changed in the program beyond enrollment, and how does that change support “a profound transformation”? I can preserve the increase from 20 to 24, but need the intended basis of this assessment to revise the whole claim faithfully.

**Preservation check:** The numbers and the transformation judgment both belong to the draft. Missing support does not make the judgment disposable. Clarification precedes a completed revision; do not invent a mechanism or reduce the sentence to its count alone.

## P08 — Repeated participial skeleton

**Trigger:** Repeat sentence-opening or sentence-ending participial frames, even with different vocabulary.

**Disallowed example:** “Drawing on X, the author... Building on Y, the author...”

**Repair:** Reorganize the propositions around their actual actors and relationships.

**Boundary:** An isolated necessary participle does not trigger this rule.

**Worked draft:** Drawing on the register, I checked the names. Building on that check, I corrected two misspellings.

**Revised prose:** I checked the names against the register and then corrected two misspellings.

**Preservation check:** The evidence source and sequence remain. The correction supplies an actual next step.

## P09 — Obscuring nominalization

**Trigger:** Stack abstract nouns and light verbs so the actor, action, or causal relation disappears.

**Disallowed example:** “The implementation of the optimization enabled the facilitation of...”

**Repair:** Recover the action and its supplied actor. Retain a technical noun if it names the actual concept.

**Boundary:** Established terms and necessary abstract concepts remain valid.

**Worked draft:** The team performed an examination of the forms and carried out the removal of duplicate fields.

**Revised prose:** The team examined the forms and removed duplicate fields.

**Preservation check:** The known actor takes the supplied actions. The revision invents no result.

## P10 — Subject-that inference shortcut

**Trigger:** Repeatedly turn a fact into an abstract character judgment or proof through subject-plus-that clauses without a warrant.

**Disallowed example:** “The fact that she returned proves her resilience.”

**Repair:** Describe the fact, then justify any inference at the appropriate strength.

**Boundary:** Ordinary that-complements and justified inferences remain valid.

**Worked draft:** The fact that she returned proves her resilience.

**Response outside the deliverable:** The draft treats the return as proof of resilience. What supplied evidence supports that judgment?

**Preservation check:** The clarification sits outside the deliverable. Do not replace a consequential character judgment with invented motivation.

## P11 — With/as descriptive tagging

**Trigger:** Habitually append with/as clauses as atmospheric or emotional labels, or imply simultaneity/causality absent from the evidence.

**Disallowed example:** “She entered, with hope in her heart, as the world shifted.”

**Repair:** Recast supplied events and emotions faithfully. Remove decorative attachment only when it carries no substantive observation. Clarify the source status of ambiguous atmosphere or implied causality.

**Boundary:** Factual accompaniment, timing, and causal relations may use with/as when accurate.

**Worked draft:** With concern filling the room, the panel postponed its vote, as the future hung in the balance. The minutes record the postponement and give no reason.

**Response outside the deliverable:** Should the description of concern count as a supplied observation, and what consequence does “the future hung in the balance” describe? The minutes record the postponement and omit its reason; that omission does not establish whether anyone expressed concern.

**Preservation check:** The response preserves the distinction between the draft's observations and the minutes' contents. It requests the information needed for a faithful recast without silently deleting the concern or inventing the stakes.

## P12 — Vague authority

**Trigger:** Attribute claims to unnamed experts, research, observers, or widespread belief without supplied support.

**Disallowed example:** “Experts agree that the shift is inevitable.”

**Repair:** Name the supplied source and its actual claim, or remove/flag the assertion.

**Boundary:** A user-supplied collective attribution can remain at its original strength.

**Worked draft:** Experts agree that the intervention prevents relapse.

**Response outside the deliverable:** Which supplied source supports the claim of expert agreement and relapse prevention?

**Preservation check:** Do not turn unsupported attribution into an unattributed medical assertion. The response does not claim a completed rewrite.

## P13 — Generic research gap

**Trigger:** Dismiss prior work and announce a gap without identifying the supplied scope and limitation.

**Disallowed example:** “Existing methods often fall short. This study addresses the gap.”

**Repair:** Specify the documented method, failure condition, and contribution.

**Boundary:** A concrete gap supported by the provided literature remains necessary.

**Worked draft:** Existing methods often fall short. Our study fills this critical gap.

**Response outside the deliverable:** Which method, documented limitation and contribution should the introduction describe?

**Preservation check:** Without that material, a polished gap statement would still lack evidence. This question stays outside finished prose.

## P14 — Promotional result

**Trigger:** Replace a measured result with unsupported praise or a promise of transformation.

**Disallowed example:** “The groundbreaking result unlocks unprecedented potential.”

**Repair:** State the measured outcome, comparison and conditions available in the source. Preserve a substantive assessment or ask for the missing benchmark. Delete promotional wording when it contributes no independent judgment or the user identifies it as unwanted material.

**Boundary:** Supported evaluations must retain their measurement basis. An unsupported claim of novelty or unprecedented performance may still carry the author's position; its missing warrant requires clarification.

**Worked draft:** The groundbreaking result unlocks unprecedented efficiency. Median processing time fell from 9 seconds to 7 seconds in the test.

**Response outside the deliverable:** What comparison supports “groundbreaking” and “unprecedented efficiency”? The test supplies a change in median processing time from 9 seconds to 7 seconds. It does not identify a benchmark for those judgments.

**Preservation check:** Preserve the measure and test setting. Clarify whether the evaluations express the author's position before deleting them; do not invent statistical significance or a wider comparison.

## P15 — Rote concession and pivot

**Trigger:** Use polite acknowledgment followed by however/yet as a reusable rebuttal frame without engaging the actual premise.

**Disallowed example:** “Your concern is valid. However, it overlooks the bigger picture.”

**Repair:** Identify the supplied concern and resolve the precise point at issue. If the author intends an assessment that the draft leaves unspecified, ask what supports it before replacing the reply.

**Boundary:** Respectful acknowledgment with substantive engagement remains appropriate.

**Worked draft:** Your scheduling concern is valid. However, it overlooks the bigger picture. The review takes two days, and we can start on Wednesday to meet Friday's deadline.

**Faithful clarification:** What broader consideration does the scheduling concern overlook? The supplied facts support starting the two-day review on Wednesday to meet Friday's deadline. They leave the broader consideration unspecified. A revision needs that point to preserve both your acknowledgment of the concern and your disagreement with it.

**Preservation check:** The schedule survives. The response identifies the missing substance behind the pivot and keeps both assessments pending clarification.

## P16 — Decorative scene opener

**Trigger:** Begin with a stock landscape, busy world, dawn, journey, or imagined scene unrelated to the supplied argument.

**Disallowed example:** “In today's rapidly evolving landscape...”

**Repair:** Begin with the actual subject, event, or problem.

**Boundary:** A supplied scene integral to a narrative may remain.

**Worked draft:** In today's rapidly evolving landscape, the library continues its journey. The reading room reopens on Monday.

**Revised prose:** The library's reading room reopens on Monday.

**Preservation check:** The concrete change supplies the opening.

## P17 — Mechanical quotation framing

**Trigger:** Place a quote at a predictable paragraph slot or make all speakers sound alike through repeated attribution and commentary.

**Disallowed example:** “As X aptly puts it... This powerful quote reminds us...”

**Repair:** Preserve exact words and source. Put the quotation where its specific evidence matters and explain that evidence.

**Boundary:** Do not rewrite quoted speech to vary its voice or evade another pattern.

**Worked task:** The author identifies the praise and general lesson as unwanted AI additions and requests a sentence reporting the source's statement.

**Worked draft:** As Neri aptly puts it, “The tally excludes refunds” (6). This powerful quote reminds us of the importance of careful interpretation.

**Revised prose:** Neri writes, “The tally excludes refunds” (6).

**Preservation check:** The supplied quotation and citation remain exact. The author authorizes removal of the added praise and lesson. If the lesson expresses the author's intended interpretation, preserve it or clarify its supporting warrant.

## P18 — Thematic overexplanation

**Trigger:** Explain the meaning of a scene after its supplied details have already conveyed it, or repeatedly restate its lesson.

**Disallowed example:** “This moment taught her the true meaning of belonging.”

**Repair:** Retain the event and remove a repeated lesson only when its substantive meaning survives elsewhere or the author requests removal. Preserve a distinct intended interpretation and identify any support it needs.

**Boundary:** Analytical discussion of a narrative can explicitly interpret evidence without circular repetition. An unsupported thematic judgment still belongs to the author until clarified or explicitly withdrawn.

**Worked draft:** Owen closed the shop and placed the key in the drawer. This act captured the enduring importance of closure.

**Faithful clarification:** What lasting significance do you intend by “the enduring importance of closure,” and which details support it? Closing the shop and putting away the key establish the actions. The thematic interpretation needs its own basis; deleting it would change the account you supplied.

**Preservation check:** The clarification retains both actions and identifies the intended judgment without substituting a weaker interpretation. The final revision awaits the author's explanation or permission to remove it.

## P19 — Unearned optimism

**Trigger:** Finish with uplift, hope, resilience, or progress unsupported by the supplied material.

**Disallowed example:** “Together, we can build a brighter future.”

**Repair:** End at the actual result, condition, or unresolved issue.

**Boundary:** State a supported positive outcome at its true scope.

**Worked task:** The author identifies the final sentence as an unwanted AI-added slogan and requests its removal.

**Worked draft:** The clinic postponed the appointment. A brighter future now awaits.

**Revised prose:** The clinic postponed the appointment.

**Preservation check:** The author explicitly authorizes removal of the slogan. The postponement survives. Without that instruction, clarify a consequential prediction or promise before deleting it.

## P20 — Forced tidy closure

**Trigger:** Claim resolution, universal significance, or agreement beyond what the argument establishes.

**Disallowed example:** “Ultimately, the debate reveals that both sides are right.”

**Repair:** Name the precise supported result or remaining premise without generic balancing.

**Boundary:** Confirmed affairs may close directly; do not manufacture uncertainty.

**Worked task:** The author asks to remove the AI-added final sentence and preserve the panel's unresolved decision.

**Worked draft:** The panel recorded two objections and deferred its decision. This brings the discussion to a satisfying conclusion.

**Revised prose:** The panel recorded two objections and deferred its decision.

**Preservation check:** The deferred decision remains open. The generic declaration of closure adds no result. A substantive claim of actual agreement would require preservation or clarification.

## P21 — Repeated syntactic or semantic skeleton

**Trigger:** Recycle a sentence frame or claim relationship under new synonyms, including cross-paragraph recurrence.

**Disallowed example:** “X reveals the complexity of Y” repeated with underscores/highlights/demonstrates.

**Repair:** Compare sentence functions as well as wording. Merge duplicates or develop a distinct inference supported by the supplied material.

**Boundary:** A repeated technical term alone does not establish a template.

**Worked draft:** The first interview lasted 30 minutes, revealing the depth of the discussion. The second interview lasted 45 minutes, highlighting the depth of the discussion.

**Response outside the deliverable:** What in the supplied interviews supports the judgment about depth? The durations alone do not establish it. I can retain both durations, but need that support to explain the judgment without repeating the same unsupported inference.

**Preservation check:** The repeated judgment may carry the author's position. Clarification preserves that possibility and avoids treating a stylistic violation as permission to delete substantive evaluation.

## P22 — Paragraph function loop

**Trigger:** Repeat definition, example, broad lesson, and recap without accumulating new argument; restate the same thesis in successive paragraphs.

**Disallowed example:** Three paragraphs each end by announcing that memory shapes identity.

**Repair:** Assign each paragraph a new warranted contribution and delete loops. Rebuild order if every paragraph returns to the same starting point.

**Boundary:** A deliberate recap may remain only when a reader needs it to follow a new consequential step.

**Worked draft:**

> The review took two days. This shows how long the review lasted.
>
> The process required two days. Overall, the review lasted two days.

**Revised prose:** The review took two days.

**Preservation check:** Both paragraphs contribute the same duration. Keeping a single statement preserves the complete factual content.
