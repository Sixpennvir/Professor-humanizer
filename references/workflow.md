# Task handling and editorial review

Read this reference after the core rules and catalogue. It applies the evidence, genre and pattern boundaries defined by those references.

## Choose the requested operation

| Operation | Inputs | Allowed work | Deliverable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Revise | A draft, audience if relevant, supplied supporting material | Improve expression and structure while preserving content and position | Final prose; note a consequential blocker separately |
| Draft | A brief and enough evidence within the authorized source boundary | Select and organize verified material; develop warranted interpretation | Prose that answers the brief |
| Diagnose | A draft and the requested review scope | Locate exact reader difficulties; explain the needed repair | Findings with excerpts, rule IDs and proposed actions |
| Review a file | Named file and scope | Read the requested content; edit only when requested | Findings or a scoped file change |

Default to revision when the user supplies prose for improvement. A request to diagnose does not authorize rewriting a file. An incomplete draft may require clarification; do not fill a gap with invented specificity.

## Separate the inputs

Keep the target draft distinct from supporting evidence and voice samples. A supplied fact may inform the target when relevant. A sample offered only to demonstrate style supplies no facts, opinions or experiences for the new text. Use its compatible diction and rhythm within the catalogue's defined boundaries.

In revision, distinguish four kinds of material:

| Material | Editorial treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Supplied factual assertion | Preserve its meaning and attribution; preserving it does not independently verify it |
| Author's interpretation or judgment | Keep its substance; improve the warrant when the supplied evidence permits |
| Empty framing or duplicate statement | Remove when it carries no independent content |
| Unsupported consequential assertion | Identify the support gap; do not silently replace the thesis or neutralize the writer |

If a source and draft disagree on a consequential fact, surface that conflict. Missing evidence does not authorize changing a promise into a weaker one, deleting an unpopular opinion, or presenting an attributed allegation as the narrator's fact.

A closed set of factual notes can rule out an added event without resolving the author's interpretation of another event. For example, an unverified stay and a judgment about resilience require separate decisions. Identify the factual gap, then preserve or clarify the judgment and its intended strength. Do not collapse both into a general instruction to remove unsupported content.

For authorized research, establish which sources the task permits and verify each added claim against them. Preserve quotations and source details. Keep research findings distinct from the supplied author's position. The original course continues to require instructor permission for outside sources under C12.

## Plan paragraph progress

For a substantial argument, record each paragraph's purpose and what the reader learns there. Use the questions below as a diagnostic. A precise inquiry may also belong in the deliverable when it organizes the analysis under R08 and P04.

- Which sentence states the paragraph's contribution?
- What supplied detail supports it?
- What inference connects that detail to the contribution?
- What new consequence or limit leads to the next paragraph?

A paragraph need not contain all four elements. A transition can span paragraphs. Avoid forcing every paragraph into an identical claim–quote–lesson sequence.

Compare non-adjacent paragraphs by proposition. Different nouns and verbs can conceal the same conclusion. Conversely, repeating a necessary technical term can help a new inference remain precise. Remove redundant propositions. Retain technical terms needed for precise reasoning.

Maintain a private claim map for a long revision. Record each distinct assertion or judgment, its support, qualifications and role in the argument. Locate it in the revision, or identify the passage that preserves it after a duplicate has been removed. Track objections and the reasons for answering them as carefully as the thesis. An unresolved support gap remains visible in the review; it cannot disappear through deletion. Return this map only when the user requests a diagnostic or preservation record.

## Revise in two directions

First read forward as the intended audience. Repair unclear references, missing logical connections, misplaced background and distracting templates.

Then compare backward against the inputs. Verify who did what, when, under which conditions, with what certainty and attribution. Inspect each quotation character for character. Check the claim map for missing objections, erased judgments, weakened recommendations and qualifications detached from the claim they limit. A smooth sentence fails if it changes a commitment or hides a limitation.

After moving or merging paragraphs, test their closing sentences against the earlier argument. Read each paragraph without its final sentence. If the remaining prose completes the new inference and the closing claim already appears elsewhere, remove that closing sentence. Keep a recap only when the next inference needs the connection it supplies. Do not preserve an old paragraph ending merely because it still reads fluently.

Do not shorten to a target compression ratio. A necessary warrant may increase length. If the task specifies a length that conflicts with preserving essential content, identify the tradeoff. Do not silently drop essential content.

## Work within the artifact

Essays and the original course assignment use continuous prose without headings or lists. Other artifacts follow the requested genre: preserve informative report headings, required sections or ordered procedural steps when the reader needs them. Documentation and diagnostic reports can use structure.

When editing a mixed file, restrict edits to the named prose. Preserve code, data, commands, URLs and citation targets. Preserve a heading that serves a required structure or anchor. If the task explicitly requires converting that artifact to continuous essay prose, identify any affected links before changing the structure. Offer a separate prose revision when it permits useful progress without mutating protected material.

## Report a diagnosis usefully

Each finding should identify a passage, explain its effect on the reader, name the relevant rule, and propose an action. Avoid long inventories of harmless words.

Example diagnostic: “The final sentence repeats the paragraph's conclusion without adding evidence (R07, P22). Delete it and let the specific limitation close the paragraph.”

For a requested audit, distinguish a mandatory rewrite from a missing-evidence blocker. No aggregate “human score” can stand in for this explanation.

## Stop at a reviewable result

Leave clear, compliant prose unchanged. Finish when the requested content survives and you have repaired the identified violations. If an unresolved constraint requires the user's decision, explain the exact conflict. Do not deliver a silent compromise or cycle through endless cosmetic variants.
