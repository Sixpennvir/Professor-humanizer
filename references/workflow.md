# Task handling and editorial review

Read this reference after the core rules and catalogue. It operationalizes the existing constraints without introducing a relaxed style profile.

## Choose the requested operation

| Operation | Inputs | Allowed work | Deliverable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Revise | A draft, audience if relevant, supplied supporting material | Improve expression and structure while preserving content and position | Final prose; note a consequential blocker separately |
| Draft | A brief and enough supplied evidence | Select and organize supplied facts; develop warranted interpretation | Prose that answers the brief |
| Diagnose | A draft and the requested review scope | Locate exact reader difficulties; explain the needed repair | Findings with excerpts, rule IDs and proposed actions |
| Review a file | Named file and scope | Read the requested content; edit only when requested | Findings or a scoped file change |

Default to revision when the user supplies prose for improvement. A request to diagnose does not authorize rewriting a file. An incomplete draft may require clarification; do not fill a gap with invented specificity.

## Separate the inputs

Keep the target draft distinct from supporting evidence and voice samples. A supplied fact may inform the target when relevant. A sample offered only to demonstrate style supplies no facts, opinions or experiences for the new text. Use its compatible diction and rhythm while retaining every strict ban.

In revision, distinguish four kinds of material:

| Material | Editorial treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Supplied factual assertion | Preserve its meaning and attribution; preserving it does not independently verify it |
| Author's interpretation or judgment | Keep its substance; improve the warrant when the supplied evidence permits |
| Empty framing or duplicate statement | Remove when it carries no independent content |
| Unsupported consequential assertion | Identify the support gap; do not silently replace the thesis or neutralize the writer |

If a source and draft disagree on a consequential fact, surface that conflict. Missing evidence does not authorize changing a promise into a weaker one, deleting an unpopular opinion, or presenting an attributed allegation as the narrator's fact.

## Plan paragraph progress

For a substantial argument, record each paragraph's purpose and what the reader learns there. Use these questions privately as a diagnostic; they do not become rhetorical questions in the deliverable.

- Which sentence states the paragraph's contribution?
- What supplied detail supports it?
- What inference connects that detail to the contribution?
- What new consequence or limit leads to the next paragraph?

A paragraph need not contain all four elements. A transition can span paragraphs. Avoid forcing every paragraph into an identical claim–quote–lesson sequence.

Compare non-adjacent paragraphs by proposition. Different nouns and verbs can conceal the same conclusion. Conversely, repeating a necessary technical term can help a new inference remain precise. Remove redundant propositions. Retain technical terms needed for precise reasoning.

## Revise in two directions

First read forward as the intended audience. Repair unclear references, missing logical connections, misplaced background and distracting templates.

Then compare backward against the inputs. Verify who did what, when, under which conditions, with what certainty and attribution. Inspect each quotation character for character. A smooth sentence fails if it changes a commitment or hides a limitation.

Do not shorten to a target compression ratio. A necessary warrant may increase length. If the task specifies a length that conflicts with preserving essential content, identify the tradeoff. Do not silently drop essential content.

## Work within the artifact

The no-heading/no-list rule applies to finished English prose. Documentation about this skill can use structure, as can a requested diagnostic report.

When editing a mixed file, restrict edits to the named prose. Preserve code, data, commands, URLs and citation targets. If removing a prose heading would break an anchor used elsewhere, identify that conflict before changing the linked structure. Offer a separate prose revision when it permits useful progress without mutating protected material. Do not quietly keep prohibited formatting and describe the result as fully compliant.

## Report a diagnosis usefully

Each finding should identify a passage, explain its effect on the reader, name the relevant rule, and propose an action. Avoid long inventories of harmless words.

Example diagnostic: “The final sentence repeats the paragraph's conclusion without adding evidence (R07, P22). Delete it and let the specific limitation close the paragraph.”

For a requested audit, distinguish a mandatory rewrite from a missing-evidence blocker. No aggregate “human score” can stand in for this explanation.

## Stop at a reviewable result

Leave clear, compliant prose unchanged. Finish when the requested content survives and you have repaired the identified violations. If an unresolved constraint requires the user's decision, explain the exact conflict. Do not deliver a silent compromise or cycle through endless cosmetic variants.
