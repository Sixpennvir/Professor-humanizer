# Writing rules

These requirements govern original English deliverables. Read the complete [pattern catalogue](patterns.md) alongside them. The [course profile](course-profile.md) adds assignment-specific constraints. Direct quotations remain protected.

## R01 — Define the task and reader

Identify what this text must accomplish and what its reader already knows. Select an exploratory, action-oriented, factual, or narrative ending based on the task. Preserve the user's scope and length; do not pad a short answer into an essay. Supply only enough background to make the next inference intelligible.

## R02 — Bound the evidence

Use only materials supplied by the user for factual claims. Do not browse to enrich a writing assignment. Research linked in this skill explains its design and supplies no subject-matter evidence for the user's text. Ask for missing material that controls the argument. You may remove an unsupported claim when removal preserves the requested meaning; otherwise flag the gap. Never invent page numbers, quotations, statistics, motives, events, or experience. Keep interpretation visibly distinct from source fact. Preserving a supplied assertion does not independently verify it. If the draft conflicts with supplied evidence, identify the conflict. Do not convert an attributed allegation into your own factual claim.

## R03 — Preserve meaning and ownership

Keep the author's position and intended degree of commitment. Preserve numbers, comparisons, exceptions, deadlines, conditions, modality, and attribution. Repair unambiguous expression directly. Clarify any proposed change to a fact, commitment, position, key qualification, or ambiguous intent. Personal voice comes from chosen details and judgments, not invented autobiography or casual filler. Use first person strategically to identify an actual interpretive move or a remaining problem. A voice sample contributes compatible expression, never its events or opinions. Distinguish empty praise from a substantive judgment; deleting an author's position requires the same care as changing it.

## R04 — Make analysis earn its place

Move from a specific detail to an explicit inference and its warrant. Explain how diction, syntax, narrative perspective, pacing, or another supplied feature affects the passage's argument. Do not merely repeat the passage or say that a word demonstrates its own topic. A proposed interpretation must withstand the surrounding text. When support runs out, narrow the claim or identify the missing premise.

## R05 — Read concepts in context

Locate a passage's function within the supplied work. Distinguish an author's claim from an example, an opponent's claim, irony, or a provisional hypothesis. The same word may express different concepts across authors. Do not transfer a definition merely because the spelling matches. Replace an unfamiliar term with an accurate expression the writer can explain; retain indispensable technical distinctions.

## R06 — Interpret charitably

Reconstruct the strongest interpretation supported by the supplied text before evaluating it. Outside the original course, criticism may proceed when the task calls for it; name the premise or inference that fails and supply the reason. In the course profile, continue the dialogue with the assigned reading and follow its restriction on arguing against the author.

## R07 — Require progress

Assign a distinct function to each paragraph. A repeated conclusion needs additional evidence, a changed scope, a tested objection, or a necessary consequence. If removing a sentence changes neither the reader's understanding nor a needed connection, remove it. Check non-adjacent repetition and summaries that repeat earlier summaries. Necessary technical terms may recur; replacing them with loose synonyms can harm precision.

## R08 — Shape continuous prose

Remove subheadings, bullets, and numbered lists from finished prose in every supported scenario. Build transitions from the actual relationship between ideas. Do not replace headings with stock signposting. Open paragraphs with a short direct claim, concrete observation, or useful imperative. Do not open with a question. Use longer sentences when the relationship requires them and shorter sentences for concentrated analysis; never alternate lengths mechanically.

## R09 — Choose an earned paragraph ending

End where the paragraph's reasoning reaches a useful result, a precise qualification, or a declarative unresolved condition. A cumulative synthesis must avoid repeated parallel clauses. A brief analogy or wry observation may work if supported and relevant. Do not require a punchline, summary, question, or transition in every paragraph. Effective departures from the paragraph template remain possible within the mandatory bans.

## R10 — Give verbs precise work

Attempt to recast all original occurrences of be, am, is, are, was, were, being, and been, including contractions where they express those forms. Inspect passive auxiliaries as well as copulas. Do not change a passive to an active construction by inventing an actor. Protect quoted text, official names, code, and required technical expressions.

Identify the action or relationship before selecting a replacement. Inspect empty uses of “have” and nominalized phrases, but retain factual possession and grammatically necessary auxiliaries. Avoid decorative verbs that change the proposition. If a faithful recasting proves unavailable, state the conflict rather than silently retaining or distorting it.

Build a small candidate verb bank before drafting when repetition seems likely. Classify candidates by meaning and evidentiary strength. “Suggest,” “demonstrate,” and “prove” cannot substitute freely. Keep an exact repeated term when the alternatives alter meaning; revise the surrounding syntax.

## R11 — Exclude formulaic constructions

Apply P01–P22 to semantic function and syntax, not just exact strings. Split-sentence and inverted variants count. Never add forced asymmetry, errors, slang, or invented quirks to disguise a template. Do not impose unrelated bans on every passive, hedge, long sentence, participle, conjunction, or punctuation mark.

## R12 — Calibrate certainty

State what the evidence supports, under what conditions, and where it stops. Avoid unsupported certainty and vague “both sides” balancing. Distinguish possibility from probability and correlation from explanation. Do not turn “may” into “will” while making a sentence shorter.

## R13 — End according to the task

Exploration: identify the precise remaining assumption, competing explanation, or evidentiary need in a declarative sentence. Do not add a generic research agenda.

Action: give the supported recommendation or next step, preserving conditions and authority. Do not manufacture a promise.

Confirmed affairs: close directly after the necessary information. Do not invent an unresolved issue.

Narrative: stop at the supported event or consequence. Do not append an explanatory moral or optimism the story has not earned.

The original course requires its specific unresolved consciousness problem. No profile permits rhetorical questions or forced closure.

## R14 — Revise by diagnosis

When asked to improve prose, identify exact places where a reader loses the argument, encounters unnecessary information, or cannot interpret a referent. Repair those places. Avoid blanket “polish” that adds ornate vocabulary. If no substantive cuts seem possible, inspect repetition and generality again; keep useful content when another pass confirms its purpose. Leave already compliant prose unchanged. Do not use reduced word count as evidence of improvement; explaining a necessary warrant may increase length.

## R15 — Protect quotations and attribution

Keep necessary supplied direct quotations exact, including banned patterns inside them. Add only source details the user actually supplied. Do not correct quotation grammar or silently paraphrase inside quotation marks. Do not move a quote to a stock location just to vary the paragraph. Retain the quote where it supports a specific inference. If the text supplies no attribution, ask for it when attribution matters.

## R16 — Separate the artifact from the review

Default output contains the requested prose alone. A requested diagnostic report may use rule IDs and structured findings outside the deliverable. Never claim that a pattern proves AI authorship or that this skill guarantees undetectable or human-quality prose. Check source fidelity before style compliance. When the two conflict, disclose the specific blocker.

## Scenario application

| Scenario | Preserve | Analysis and progress | Ending |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Academic close reading | Passage, context, citations, interpretive scope | Explain a formal detail and its effect | Precise unresolved premise where warranted |
| Research summary | Supplied methods, results, limitations | Explain what the supplied study supports | Supported finding and actual limit |
| Professional email | Recipient, action, owner, date, commitment | Remove ceremonial framing; clarify the request | Supported next step or direct close |
| Decision memo | Options, criteria, supplied evidence | Justify a decision without parallel sales slogans | Recommendation with real conditions |
| Public explanation | Reader knowledge, terms, factual boundaries | Develop one relation at a time | Implication within the evidence |
| Narrative | Events, perspective, supplied voice | Let detail carry meaning without thematic repetition | Earned event or consequence |

Every row retains the same mandatory style bans. Scenario adaptation changes the rhetorical task, never the source boundary.

## Candidate verbs from the supplied writing exercise

This bank preserves the source exercise's options; none carries an automatic recommendation. Choose by the action and warrant.

- Observation or depiction: exhibit, portray, display, delineate, present, express, illustrate, trace, sketch a profile of, outline the contours of.
- Tentative inference: lead one to suspect, lead one to think, lead one to infer, hint at, intimate, suggest, convey an impression, constitute signs or traces of.
- Evidence or confirmation: serve as evidence for, demonstrate, confirm, establish, prove, make evident or clear. The last five require appropriately strong support.
- Disclosure or emphasis: betray, expose, uncover, disclose, reveal, unveil, lay bare, bring to light, allow something to stand out or come to light, reveal the inner workings of, lay open the assumption that, bring out the nuances of, cast a sharp light on, throw into relief, flag.
- Boundary or development: delimit, demarcate, chart the bounds of, yield, unfold, lead one to conclude.
- Attention or display: put on view, parade, put on display, flaunt, catch the eye, catch the ear, catch the attention.

Many multiword options will sound excessive in a particular sentence. The exercise asks for a precise action; it does not require ornamental variation.
